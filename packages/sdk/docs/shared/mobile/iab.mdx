## Prepare your Inth project

Configure an IAB policy in your Inth project for regions using TCF. The policy must serve the current Global Vendor List (GVL) and disclosures for every Google Additional Consent provider you offer. A category-only policy does not supply vendor disclosures.

Set the CMP ID and internal CMP version assigned to your deployment (separate from your app and SDK versions), your two-letter publisher country code, and a consent language matching your disclosure text. For Google's publisher App review, include Google vendor `755` and your Additional Consent providers.

## Present the consent flow

Use the native presenter from your platform quickstart. The stock flow covers purpose and vendor consent, legitimate-interest objections, special features, publisher restrictions, Additional Consent providers, and custom partners. Keep a Privacy settings entry point available after dismissal so users can update or withdraw consent.

In React Native, you can also build custom IAB screens with `useIAB()` and `useTCString()`, backed by native validation and commits; see [Build your own IAB screen](/docs/frameworks/react-native/headless#build-your-own-iab-screen).

### Custom partners

Custom vendors configured in your Inth policy appear on a dedicated Custom Partners page in the stock flow, separate from IAB partners. Accept all grants them and Reject all withdraws them.

Because the TC String encodes IAB vendors only, check custom partner permissions with `isCustomVendorAllowed(id)` (string ID) on Swift and Kotlin. In React Native, read `useIABSnapshot()?.customVendors` and update choices through `useIAB()` (`setVendorConsent` and `setVendorLegitimateInterest`).

Draft edits are not active consent until saved. If vendor disclosures change while the visitor is editing, keep the draft visible and ask them to review the updated list.

## Gate ad integrations

Disable automatic startup on your ad SDKs. Before initializing an SDK or requesting ads, check the native gate for the vendor, purpose, and legal basis:

- **TCF vendors.** `isIABAllowed` (Swift) or `isIabAllowed` (Kotlin).
- **Google Additional Consent.** `isAdTechProviderAllowed` (requires TCF consent for purposes 1, 3, and 4).
- **Custom partners.** `isCustomVendorAllowed(id)`.
- **Derived categories.** `iabPurposeCategories()` maps committed IAB and custom partner choices to Inth categories.

Keep gate subscriptions active so withdrawals stop or reconfigure ad collection. A category grant or non-empty TC String does not replace these checks.

## Inspect platform signals

Platform adapters mirror committed signals into standard `UserDefaults` on iOS and default `SharedPreferences` on Android while keeping protected storage as the authority:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `IABTCF_TCString` | Encoded native TCF decision |
| `IABTCF_gdprApplies` | Platform integer indicating GDPR applicability |
| Purpose and vendor consent fields | Committed consent bit vectors |
| Purpose and vendor LI fields | Legitimate-interest bit vectors |
| `IABTCF_AddtlConsent` | Additional Consent v2 provider selections |

Never write these keys manually or cache them in JavaScript as permanent authority; resets, identity changes, and expired disclosures invalidate them.

## Keep ATT separate

On iOS, App Tracking Transparency (ATT) authorizes cross-app tracking. Request ATT only if your app tracks users, with a clear usage description. An ATT grant never grants Inth category or vendor consent, and Inth consent never bypasses ATT denial. Android has no ATT prompt.

In React Native, `useTrackingAuthorization()` reads ATT status and `useIsTrackingAllowed(category)` combines ATT with category consent. Neither replaces IAB vendor and purpose checks.

## Ask for ATT after the consent prompt

When ATT is enabled (`enableAppTrackingTransparency` in Expo or `NSUserTrackingUsageDescription` in `Info.plist`), the stock prompt coordinates Apple's ATT alert. By default (`afterConsent`), the consent prompt appears first, and Apple's alert follows only if the visitor granted marketing.

Configure the timing with `trackingRequest` on `<InthProvider>` in React Native, or on `.inth(...)` and `ConsentRootViewController` in Swift:

| Value | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `afterConsent` (default) | Shows the consent prompt first, then Apple's ATT alert after dismissal only if marketing is granted. |
| `beforeConsent` | Shows Apple's ATT alert before the consent prompt. Not recommended; use only if product or legal review requires it. |
| `manual` | No automatic ATT request. Call `requestTrackingAuthorization()`, `useTrackingRequest()`, or `InthApple.tracking.request` directly. |

The SDK requests ATT only when ATT is enabled, Apple's status is not determined, marketing is not refused (by a stored choice, GPC, or TC String), the app is foregrounded, no Inth screen is open, and an Inth root is mounted. Under `afterConsent`, ATT fires after closing a prompt with marketing granted, on a later launch where stored consent already grants marketing and ATT is undetermined, or after enabling marketing in privacy settings. With `autoPresent` off, nothing fires at launch, and custom JS consent UIs must call `useTrackingRequest()` or `requestTrackingAuthorization()` themselves.

With `beforeConsent`, Apple's alert runs right before an owed or programmatically opened consent prompt (or at launch if no prompt is owed and marketing is not refused), at most once per launch, and never delays user-opened privacy settings.

On Apple's expanded EU ATT sheet (shown in Germany, France, Italy, Poland, Romania, and the rest of the EU when preferred; optional rich copy via `NSUserTrackingMarkdownUsageDescription` or `trackingMarkdownUsageDescription`), tapping Additional Information opens Inth's privacy settings. Custom UIs receive this button by passing `onTrackingAdditionalInformation` on `<InthProvider>` or `onAdditionalInformation:` in Swift.

### Ask again after a year (EU)

In the EU, Apple allows re-prompting ATT one year after a previous answer. Enable it with `trackingReprompt="yearly"` on `<InthProvider>` or `trackingReprompt: .yearly` in Swift (defaults to `never`). When enabled, the SDK re-requests once per launch if status is `authorized` or `denied`, marketing is granted, and 365 days have passed since the last recorded answer. It never re-prompts when marketing is refused, tracking is restricted, or `trackingRequest` is `manual`. Android ignores `trackingRequest` and `trackingReprompt`.

### ATT and IAB TCF

ATT and TCF are independent gates, and tracking requires both. Refusal in either blocks tracking; the SDK never writes ATT status into the TC String or alters ATT based on TCF.

Under an IAB TCF policy, the after-consent ATT check treats `marketing` as consented when every TCF purpose among 2, 3, and 4 requested by any vendor (or custom partner) is consented, at least one vendor, custom partner, or Google ad tech provider can track, and Inth's `marketing` category is granted. Accept all triggers ATT; Reject all never does.

Apple's [user privacy and data use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/) guidance advises against showing ATT to users who already opted out in a consent prompt, and App Review Guidelines 5.1.1(iv) and 5.1.2(i) prohibit forcing or rewarding tracking consent.
